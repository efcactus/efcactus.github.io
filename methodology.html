Our methodology

From understanding to implementation. From complexity to a working result.

Complex problems are rarely solved by addressing their most visible symptoms. They require an understanding of the environment in which they arise, the relationships between their constituent parts, and the consequences of the decisions made along the way.

EF Cactus approaches assignments as an engineering process: understand the domain, establish the real problem, develop the solution and carry it through implementation.

01

Understand the domain

You cannot engineer a sound solution without understanding the environment in which it must operate.

The starting point is understanding the substance of the business activity and the disciplines involved.

This may involve commercial arrangements, financial products, contractual obligations, legislation and regulation, organisational structures, operating processes or technology.

We examine not only what an organisation does, but why it does it, how it works, what governs it and what constraints apply.

The objective is to establish a sufficiently complete understanding of the domain before deciding what needs to change.

02

Decompose the problem

Complexity becomes manageable when its constituent elements and relationships are understood.

A problem that initially appears to be one issue may contain several distinct problems, dependencies and conflicting requirements.

We break it down into its constituent elements, identifying:

  • The underlying business requirement.
  • Applicable legal, regulatory and contractual obligations.
  • Operational and organisational dependencies.
  • Financial and commercial considerations.
  • Technology requirements and limitations.
  • Risks, constraints and decision points.

This creates a structured understanding of the problem and distinguishes essential requirements from assumptions, inherited practices and unnecessary complexity.

03

Engineer the solution

The objective is not simply to recommend a course of action, but to design something that can work.

With the problem understood and decomposed, the next step is to develop the solution.

This may involve designing a business or commercial model, structuring a financial product, developing contractual arrangements, engineering operational procedures, defining governance, specifying technology requirements or combining several of these elements.

The solution must be internally coherent. Its commercial rationale, contractual basis, regulatory obligations, operating arrangements and technical implementation must support one another.

Where appropriate, we challenge the original design rather than merely adding controls around it.

04

Manage implementation

A sound design has little value if it cannot be translated into effective delivery.

Implementation requires more than a plan. It requires the solution to be translated into defined activities, responsibilities, dependencies, deliverables and decisions.

EF Cactus applies structured project and programme management to take the work forward.

  • Defining scope and deliverables.
  • Developing work breakdown structures and implementation plans.
  • Establishing responsibilities and decision rights.
  • Managing dependencies and critical paths.
  • Coordinating business, professional and technical work.
  • Monitoring progress, resolving issues and maintaining delivery control.

The emphasis is on maintaining the integrity of the original solution throughout implementation.

05

Make it work

Completion is not the production of documents or the closure of a project. It is the achievement of the intended result.

The final test is whether the solution works in its intended environment.

Processes must operate. Responsibilities must be understood. Systems must perform their required functions. Contractual and regulatory requirements must be reflected in actual practice. People must be able to carry out their roles.

We examine the outcome against the original objectives and address gaps between design and operational reality.

Where necessary, this includes refinement, testing, training, knowledge transfer and the establishment of ongoing controls.

The objective is a functioning result, not simply a completed assignment.

A guiding principle

Elimination Before Mitigation

Do not spend resources managing the consequences of complexity that should never have been introduced.

At every stage, we question whether an arrangement, dependency, process or technology is genuinely necessary.

Risk controls have their place, but they should not become a substitute for sound initial decisions.
